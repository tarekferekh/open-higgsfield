# FityZone Brand System

Read `marketing/brand-profile.md` before starting.

## Evidence workflow

1. Inventory the available sources: approved brand bible, vector logos, owned social posts, website, owner decisions, and analytics.
2. Build an evidence table with the source, date, observation, and confidence.
3. Mark every conclusion as **verified**, **observed**, or **proposed**.
4. Follow this authority order: approved brand bible, owner decision, owned-channel evidence, analytics, then proposal.
5. Never convert an inference into a brand fact without approval.
6. Update the brand profile only with durable verified decisions.

## Extract the system

Capture:

- positioning, promise, audience, and differentiators;
- logo variants, clear space, minimum size, and misuse rules;
- primary, secondary, neutral, and accessibility colors with exact values;
- Arabic and Latin typography, hierarchy, weight, spacing, and fallback fonts;
- imagery, lighting, composition, people, environments, and AI-image policy;
- grid, spacing, shapes, icons, motion, transitions, and watermark behavior;
- vocabulary, tone, banned phrases, claims, proof, and calls to action.

When evidence is incomplete, produce a gap list and a proposed direction separately.

## Design deliverables

For each post, carousel, Story, cover, or Reel, provide:

- objective and audience;
- canvas and safe-area dimensions;
- layout hierarchy;
- exact copy and line breaks;
- asset list;
- color and type tokens from the approved profile;
- image or video-generation prompt;
- accessibility notes;
- export names and formats.

Use 1080 × 1350 for portrait feed posts and 1080 × 1920 for Stories or Reels unless the platform requirements have changed and are verified.

Use generative images for scenes and concepts, not for exact logo reproduction or typography. Place approved vector logos and final text during layout production.

## Quality gate

Reject work that has unverified brand claims, improvised logo treatment, unreadable mobile text, weak contrast, cluttered bilingual copy, inconsistent visual language, fabricated testimonials, or a visual idea that cannot be produced reliably.
